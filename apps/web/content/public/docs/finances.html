finances: understand what is yours to use

finances brings accounts, transactions, spending, and planning into one workspace. it helps you understand what happened, what belongs to you, and what a plan depends on. it does not move money or replace your bank.

what you can do

  • complete or resume setup, connect supported financial accounts, and maintain manual accounts or imported records where supported.
  • inspect accounts, balances, ownership, and planning inclusion. keep a connected account’s provider evidence separate from your own classification choices.
  • review transactions and spending; inspect full outflow as well as personal cost. correct categories and review transfers, reimbursements, merchants, and related records through the available workflows.
  • inspect cash flow and wealth summaries. check freshness and underlying records before relying on a total.
  • review a proposed budget, its resources, allocations, assumptions, and reasoning. approve an exact plan or revision yourself.
  • manage supported goals, budget buckets, recurring items, and rules. use chat, reviews, automations, and activity to connect questions and decisions to their records.

what an agent can do

an authorized agent can inspect financial reports and transaction details, help classify activity, prepare budget proposals, compare scenarios, and surface questions that need your judgment. supported tools also cover accounts, categories, merchants, goals, recurring items, review workflows, maintenance, and recovery. available mutations depend on grants, review requirements, and the operation’s policy.

active budget activation and revision require your exact authenticated approval. execution permissions or a conversational “yes” do not substitute for that approval. an agent can help start a bank-connection handoff, but you complete the provider’s login and verification yourself.

why it works this way

money leaving an account is not always your final personal cost. a transfer, a shared purchase, and a reimbursable expense need different treatment. expected reimbursement is not received money. separating those meanings makes spending and goal progress more useful while keeping uncertainty visible.

plans are proposals grounded in current evidence. you should be able to inspect assumptions, answer an ambiguous transaction question, and see how a revision changes the plan before approving it. a preview of a policy is not proof that automatic reallocation or an external payment occurred.

try this: “review the production expenses, flag unclear transfers and reimbursements, and propose a budget for the next project. show your assumptions and leave the plan for my approval.” you can inspect and correct the same records directly in finances.
