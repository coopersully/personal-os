Your data and agent access

Understand what you connect, what you share, and how to stop access.

What lives in nohmi

nohmi stores material you create and information synchronized from services you connect. Mail, events, tasks, financial records, personal context, and action history support the workspaces you use. A synchronized record may be a local copy; changing or deleting it can have different consequences from changing the original provider record. Review the action before confirming it.

What an agent can receive

When you connect an agent, the permissions you grant determine which supported records it can read and which actions it can request. Agent access can expose sensitive information, including message contents, financial context, and personal notes. An external agent or AI host processes the information it receives under its own policies. Check those policies, including retention and training settings, before connecting it.

nohmi does not sell your personal data for model training. External agent providers set their own retention and model-training practices. Review their policies and account settings before sharing data, and choose a provider whose practices fit the information you plan to share.

Your controls

  • Review connected agents and their permissions in Settings, and revoke access you no longer need.
  • Review connection controls in each workspace and revoke provider authorization at the provider when needed.
  • Inspect recorded activity and confirm consequential results with the relevant provider.
  • Disconnect your phone in Texting or reply STOP to block program texts.
  • Contact support for access, correction, export availability, or account deletion requests.

Disconnecting is different from deleting

Revoking a connection does not retract data already delivered to a provider or agent. Disconnecting your number does not erase conversation or consent history. Account deletion requests may require identity verification and may be subject to security, legal, backup, and opt-out retention needs. See the Privacy policy for the governing disclosures.

Be thoughtful about notifications

SMS and device notifications can reveal information to anyone who can see your phone. Text messages may include context about your work, such as a task, event, or merchant name. Avoid sending passwords, verification codes, full financial account numbers, or material you would not want visible on a lock screen.
